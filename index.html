Evidentiary Standard vs. Burden Level

Comparison of judicial findings needed to compel platform disclosures

Tier 1: Basic Subscriber Info (BSI) 18 U.S.C. § 2703(c)(2) • Subpoena Threshold

Requires administrative, grand jury, or trial subpoena. No prior judicial review required. Covers account holder name, duration of service, billing address, signup IP with timestamp, and payment source connection details.

Tier 2: Non-Content Metadata 18 U.S.C. § 2703(d) • Specific & Articulable Facts

Requires court order. Law enforcement must offer specific and articulable facts showing reasonable grounds that records are relevant and material to an ongoing criminal investigation. Covers message headers, IP connection logs, and audit trails.

Tier 3: Stored Communication Contents 18 U.S.C. § 2703(a) & 4th Amendment • Probable Cause Warrant

Requires Rule 41 search warrant backed by probable cause finding by a magistrate judge. Applies to private messages, email text, uploaded cloud files, and AI model prompts and chat outputs.

Civil Discovery Preclusion 18 U.S.C. § 2702 • Statutory Civil Bar

Third-party providers are statutorily prohibited from disclosing content to civil litigants. Direct Rule 45 subpoenas on providers for DMs, emails, or chat logs are routinely quashed.

⚖️ Pivotal Jurisprudential Precedents

United States v. Warshak

6th Cir. 2010

Established that internet users hold a reasonable expectation of privacy under the 4th Amendment in stored email contents, requiring a search warrant based on probable cause.

Carpenter v. United States

U.S. 2018

Curtailed the Third-Party Doctrine, holding that acquiring historical location records provides an intimate window into personal life and constitutes a search requiring a warrant.

United States v. Hoehner

D. Me. 2025

Confirmed that federal authorities must obtain a Rule 41 search warrant supported by probable cause to compel generative AI providers (OpenAI) to disclose stored chat transcripts.
